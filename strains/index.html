Flowerhorn strains, and how to tell them apart

Kamfa, ZZ, SRD, Thai Silk: the names on a flowerhorn tank tell you something, but never everything. Here is how the strains differ and what to look at first.

What “strain” means for a flowerhorn

A flowerhorn strain is a line of fish bred, generation after generation, toward a particular look: a square body, heavy pearls, solid red, a silver sheen. It is not a breed in the dog-show sense. There is no registry, no written standard, and no pedigree papers. The names come from breeders and sellers, and they spread by word of mouth.

Because every flowerhorn is a hybrid (see where the flowerhorn came from), strains overlap constantly. A fish can carry Kamfa structure and Red Dragon color at the same time, and many do. Think of strains as families with recognizable faces rather than sealed boxes.

The founding lines

Reference sources usually group the early derived flowerhorns into four main varieties: Zhen Zhu (the pearl flowerhorn), Kamfa, Golden Monkey, and the Golden Base group, which includes the faders. Accounts disagree on which came first, and dates vary by who tells the story, so treat any precise timeline with caution.

Most of what you see for sale today was built on those foundations. Red Dragon and Super Red Dragon are color lines selected mostly from ZZ-type fish. Kamfa was pushed further with Vieja and parrot-type crosses. Thai Silk, Kamalau, King Kamfa and the short-body fish came later still, each selected for one standout trait.

How to tell the major strains apart

Color is the least reliable clue, because it changes with age, diet and mood. Start with structure: body outline, eyes, mouth and how the fins sit against the tail. The table below describes typical fish; individual fish, and especially crosses, will break the pattern.

Why the names get confusing

Strain names stack. SRD ZZ means a ZZ-type fish graded as Super Red Dragon for color; SRD Kamfa is a Kamfa-structured fish with that same deep red. The same fish might be sold as Kamfa in one shop and KML in the next.

  • One name, several meanings: Kamalau is described as a Kamfa and Malau cross in some sources and used for Golden Monkey in others.
  • Several names, one fish: Kamfa is also sold as Jin Hua, and Thai Silk is often marketed as Titanium.
  • Labels that aren’t strains: F0 to F4 are generation claims for Kamfa, not separate strains. The breeding page and glossary explain the terms.
  • Trade names: sellers coin new names for fish with little real difference to stand out.

Our advice at the counter is always the same: buy the fish in front of you, not the label on the tank.

Questions about flowerhorn strains

How many types of flowerhorn are there?

There is no fixed number, because breeders keep crossing lines and coining names. Most fish sold today fall into a handful of families: Kamfa, Zhen Zhu, Red Dragon, Thai Silk, Golden Base, Kamalau and short body.

What is the difference between a Kamfa and a ZZ?

A Kamfa usually has a square body, even lips, sunken white or yellow eyes and fins that wrap the tail. A ZZ is longer, with a jutting lower jaw, red bulging eyes, a rounded tail and heavier pearling.

Is SRD a separate strain?

Not really. Super Red Dragon is a higher color grade of Red Dragon, and the label is usually paired with a body type, as in SRD ZZ or SRD Kamfa.

Which flowerhorn strain is best for a beginner?

ZZ and Golden Base fish are generally the hardiest and most forgiving. Kamfa lines are often described as more sensitive to water changes and swings in conditions.
