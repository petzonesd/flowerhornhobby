Kamalau flowerhorns

Take a Kamfa and give it a thicker, pouting “monkey” face, a neater kok and a frosting of pearls. That is the Kamalau, a cross whose name is almost as tangled as its family tree.

What a Kamalau is

A Kamalau is a cross between two flowerhorn lines. The parentage most often given, including in the Wikipedia summary of flowerhorn varieties and in many retailer descriptions, is a Kamfa male with a Malau female. You will see the same fish sold as Kamfa Malau, Kamfamalau, or shortened to KML.

The goal of the cross is easy to describe. Breeders want the Kamfa’s square body, big kok and compact face, combined with the Malau’s thick-lipped “monkey” expression, warm color and pearled head. When it works, the result reads as a Kamfa at first glance and something a little different on the second.

Hedge the pedigree

Not every source agrees on the cross. Some hobby articles reverse the sexes, and some forum breeders describe Kamalau lines built from ZZ and Kamfa stock. Treat the parentage as the usual recipe, not a rule.

As with every flowerhorn “strain,” there is no registry. A Kamalau label tells you what the seller believes the parents were, and its value depends on whether the fish shows the traits. Our strains overview explains why these names work better as families than as breeds.

The name, and why it causes confusion

Most of the naming tangle makes sense once you know that these words come from Cantonese, the language of many Malaysian Chinese breeders.

  • Malau comes from 馬騮, a Cantonese word for monkey. It refers to the face: puffy lips, a short nose and full cheeks.
  • Kam is the Cantonese reading of 金, gold, commonly said to be the same syllable that starts “Kamfa.”
  • Kamalau, 金馬騮, therefore reads as “golden monkey.”

So the same word works two ways. Read as a contraction, Kamalau means a Kamfa crossed with a Malau. Read as plain Cantonese, it is simply the name Golden Monkey, which also belongs to an older, famous Malaysian line. Hobby writers have used the terms both ways for years, which is why you will find one source calling the Golden Monkey and Kamalau the same fish and another treating the Kamalau as a later cross. Both are describing real usage.

The Malau and Golden Monkey background

The Malau side of the family traces back, in hobby accounts, to the original Golden Monkey flowerhorns bred in Malaysia. Blog histories place that line with breeders in Ipoh and describe it as a prized fish that proved very difficult to reproduce true, even for the people who created it. It is also the line behind the record-setting sale noted in our origins timeline.

Accounts of the original Golden Monkey describe a fish with:

  • deep red color with a smooth gradation into the body color
  • wormy, heavy pearling over the head
  • a thick, chubby face, not the “baby face” of a Kamfa
  • a broad, muscular body and a firmer tail than a typical Zhen Zhu
  • inset red eyes and flower markings that are often broken or faint

Once the original fish were gone, “Malau” drifted from a single line toward a type: any flowerhorn bred to show that monkey face. Some breeders describe modern Malau as coming from ZZ stock with Vieja influence, split into ZZ-type Malau and the Kamfa-type fish covered on this page. Breeders also commonly describe Malau fish as slow to mature, with square gill plates and a face that takes time to fill out. For the ZZ side of that history, see our Zhen Zhu guide. The gold faders that also get called Golden Monkey are a different story, covered on our Golden Base page.

What a good Kamalau looks like

Descriptions vary by breeder, but these are the traits keepers and sellers most often use to judge a Kamalau:

BodyKamfa-like: compact and deep, but often described as more muscular and proportional than a very short Kamfa.
FaceThe defining trait. Thick cheeks and gill plates, fuller lips and a short nose that give a pouting “monkey” expression.
EyesUsually red or orange, often set in like a Kamfa’s.
KokKamfa-style and often large, but judged on shape: neat and well rounded, sometimes with a slight mushroom profile.
PearlingHeavy and expected. A frosted layer of pearls over the kok and face is one of the traits buyers look for.
ColorWarm reds, oranges and yellows. Solid red on the head and chest is generally preferred.
DevelopmentOften a slower starter. Young fish can look plain next to Kamfa juveniles and improve with age.

That last row matters for buyers. A Kamalau that looks ordinary at 3 inches (8 cm) may turn into the best fish in the tank at a year old, and the reverse happens too. For a general checklist of what to judge at any age, see our grading guide.

Kamalau vs Kamfa: how to tell them apart

Because one parent is a Kamfa, the two can be very close, and even experienced keepers say that telling them apart takes years of looking at fish in person. These are the differences people point to most:

KamfaKamalau
Cheeks & gill platesSlimmer, neaterThick, chubby, often squarer
Lips & mouthThinner, even lipsFuller lips with a pout
EyesTraditionally white, yellow or orange, sometimes red; sunken look prizedMost often red or orange, inset
KokVery large, often a soft water headOften large, with the emphasis on a neat, rounded shape
Head pearlingDense pearling on body and facePearls expected across the kok, often a frosted look
Growing upBody shape usually shows earlyCan look average young and fill out later

One more point of confusion: the F0 to F4 labels describe generations of Kamfa. A seller may tell you the Kamfa parent’s generation, but those labels say nothing about the Malau side. Our Kamfa guide covers the father’s side of the cross in depth.

Buying and keeping a Kamalau

Care is the same as for any flowerhorn. An adult needs its own tank of at least 75 gallons, strong filtration, 80–86°F (27–30°C) water, big weekly water changes and a quality pellet diet. Start with our flowerhorn care guide and the kok growth guide, since a neat, well-filled kok is a big part of what you pay for.

When shopping, a few habits help:

  • Ask about the parents. Photos of the Kamfa male and Malau female as adults are the best evidence you are getting what the label says.
  • Judge the face. If a young fish has no hint of thicker lips or fuller cheeks, the “Malau” part of the name may be optimistic.
  • Give it time. Kamalau fish often reward patience. Don’t write off a slow starter at six months.
  • Buy from someone who stands behind the fish. Our current stock, when we have Kamalau and related lines, is listed at flowerhorn.co.

Questions about Kamalau flowerhorns

What is a Kamalau flowerhorn?

A Kamalau is usually described as a cross of a Kamfa male and a Malau female. It combines a Kamfa-type body and kok with the Malau’s thick-lipped “monkey” face, red eyes and pearled head.

Is Kamalau the same as Golden Monkey?

Sometimes. In Cantonese, Kamalau literally means golden monkey, and some sources use the names for the same fish, while others use Kamalau specifically for the Kamfa x Malau cross.

What is the difference between Kamalau and Kamfa?

A Kamalau typically has thicker cheeks, fuller pouting lips, red or orange eyes and a neater kok with frosted pearling. A Kamfa has a slimmer face, often pale eyes and is prized for a very large kok.

What does Malau mean in flowerhorns?

Malau comes from a Cantonese word for monkey and refers to the puffy-lipped, short-nosed face bred into these lines. It traces back to the Golden Monkey flowerhorns developed in Malaysia.
