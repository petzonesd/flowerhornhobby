Hybrid cichlids

Hybrid cichlids explained

The flowerhorn and the blood parrot are not species. They are crosses built by breeders, and they sit at the center of one of the oldest arguments in fishkeeping.

What makes a fish a hybrid

A hybrid is the offspring of parents from two different species. Many cichlids are closely related enough to spawn with each other when they share a tank, even species that live in different countries and would never meet in the wild. Breeders learned to use that on purpose.

The hybrids in this section are not one-off accidents. They come from lines that have been crossed, re-crossed and selected for decades, mostly from Central American cichlids such as the Midas, the red devil, the trimac and the redhead. Nobody published the recipes, so exact ancestry is mostly hobby lore, and we flag it that way.

Why Asian breeders create them

Taiwan produced the blood parrot in the 1980s, and Malaysian breeders used it and other cichlids to build the flowerhorn in the 1990s. The motive was the market. Buyers in Chinese-speaking communities pay for red and gold color, a round body and a domed forehead, all associated with luck and prosperity (see flowerhorns and feng shui). A new look sells, and a cross is the fastest way to get one.

A cross can also combine one parent’s color with another’s head or body, and some lines were bred smaller or calmer to suit home tanks. The full story is on our flowerhorn origins page.

Can hybrid cichlids breed?

Often, but unevenly. Male blood parrots are generally infertile, most King Kong parrot males are reported to be sterile, and many flowerhorn males never fertilize a clutch. Females are a different story. Female parrots and flowerhorns are frequently fertile and will spawn with other cichlids, which is how the flowerhorn and the Kirin parrot were built in the first place.

So a pair of parrots will often lay and guard eggs that simply turn white, while a female hybrid in a mixed cichlid tank can surprise you with fry. See how flowerhorns are bred for the flowerhorn side.

Name check

The aquarium “parrot cichlid” is unrelated to the marine parrotfish. The name comes from its beak-like mouth.

The ethics debate

Few topics split cichlid keepers like hybrids. Both sides have a point.

The purist case

  • Genetic pollution. A second-generation hybrid can look almost exactly like a pure species. Sold under a species name, it can contaminate lines hobbyists keep pure.
  • Deformities. The blood parrot’s mouth cannot close fully, and short-body lines are prone to spine and swim bladder problems.
  • What the trade encourages. Dyed, tattooed and tail-cut parrots, and huge numbers of culls.

The keeper’s case

  • Bred for tanks. No wild fish are collected to supply them, and they are hardy in aquariums.
  • Great pets. Flowerhorns and parrots are among the most interactive fish in the hobby, and they bring many people into fishkeeping.
  • They already exist. A healthy, well-kept hybrid can live ten years or more.

Where we land, as people who keep and sell them: label hybrids honestly, never pass one off as a species, keep them out of species breeding programs, and don’t buy dyed or mutilated fish.

Never release a hybrid

Flowerhorns dumped during the Asian boom now live wild in Malaysia and Singapore, where they compete with and eat native fish, and Australia bans their import. South Florida already hosts established nonnative cichlids such as the Mayan cichlid. If you can’t keep yours, ask a local fish store or aquarium club to help rehome it. Releasing aquarium fish is harmful everywhere and illegal in many states.

The hybrids you will see in US stores

  • Flowerhorn. The big one: a Malaysian hybrid with a kok, bold markings and a dog-like personality. Start with the strain guide.
  • Blood parrot. Round, orange-red, with a small beak-like mouth. The classic peaceful-ish parrot.
  • King Kong parrot. A larger, more robust parrot type with a better-working mouth and sometimes a hump.
  • Kirin parrot. Usually described as a flowerhorn and blood parrot cross, with parrot shape and flowerhorn color.
  • Others, briefly. Platinum (white) and panda (black and white) parrots are color forms of the parrot hybrid. The polar blue parrot is sold as a blood parrot and convict cross. The red Texas is a cross of Herichthys and Amphilophus-type parents. Yuan Bao or ingot parrots are extra-round premium parrot types.

Questions about hybrid cichlids

Is a flowerhorn a hybrid?

Yes. The flowerhorn was developed in Malaysia in the 1990s from Central American cichlids, chiefly the red devil and trimac, along with the blood parrot, itself a hybrid.

Are hybrid cichlids bad?

They are controversial. Purists object to deformities and to hybrids contaminating pure species lines, while many keepers value them as hardy, interactive pets. Honest labeling and never releasing them address the biggest risks.

Can hybrid cichlids reproduce?

Many can. Males of blood parrots, King Kong parrots and flowerhorns are often sterile, but females are frequently fertile and can spawn with other cichlids.

What is the most popular hybrid cichlid?

In US stores the blood parrot is the most widely sold, while the flowerhorn has the largest dedicated following of keepers and breeders.
