Feng shui luck score

The old readings on kok, color, pearling, markings and placement, turned into one number — for fun, not fortune-telling.

Score your fish

Flowerhorns have been read as lucky fish since the 1990s — the kok compared to the God of Longevity, the markings searched for characters and numbers, the colors read for wealth and joy. This runs those same old signs through one weighted score, the way we would eyeball a fish in the store. Answer what you can actually see.

Feng shui luck score

Answer for the fish in front of you. Nothing here is saved or uploaded.

The signs

Kok size and shape

The single biggest factor. Its resemblance to Shou Xing, the God of Longevity, is the whole reason the kok matters in feng shui at all.

Base color

Red reads as luck and vitality, gold as wealth, silver as clarity. None of it is bad fortune — just a different reading.

Pearling

Read as scattered coins or jewels — the sparkle version of abundance.

Markings (the “flower”)

No two fish share a flower. A pattern people actually read as a character or a number is the rare, legendary case.

Tank placement

Practitioners disagree on the details, but north and southeast come up most often. See placement room by room for the full picture.

Kept alone?

A lone flowerhorn is traditionally read as a guardian presence holding its own territory.

Water clarity right now

The one rule every tradition and every fishkeeper agrees on. It caps the score below, on purpose.

    Why these signs, in the old reading

    • Clean water outranks everything. Every tradition, and every fishkeeper, agrees on this one. The score is built to prove it.
    • The kok carries the most weight. Its resemblance to Shou Xing, the God of Longevity, is the original reason the flowerhorn is considered lucky at all.
    • Markings are the wildcard. A pattern read as a character or number is rare enough that it is worth the biggest single bonus after the kok.
    • Color and pearling add up, but gently. Red, gold, silver and heavy pearling are all read as fortunate — none of them are read as unlucky.

    Questions

    Is this a real feng shui reading?

    No. It is a fun way to combine the folklore explained on our feng shui page into one score. For advice on your actual home, a practitioner who knows your floor plan and birth chart is the real source, not a web calculator.

    Why does dirty water cap the score so hard?

    Every feng shui tradition, and every fishkeeper, agrees on one rule above all others: clean water matters more than any marking or color. A fish in cloudy water is not read as lucky by any account, so the score is capped until the water is clean.

    What if my flowerhorn's markings do not look like anything?

    That is most flowerhorns. A recognizable character or number is the legendary exception the old lottery-number stories were about, not the rule, and plenty of low-scoring fish here still make excellent pets.
